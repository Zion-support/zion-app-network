Put the guide into practice

  1. Fix the document version and reviewer

    Record the file name, version, review purpose and person authorized to decide. Use a redacted or synthetic sample until handling of the real document is approved.

  2. Link observations to evidence

    For each clause or status, record the exact source location, observation and unresolved question. Compare results against the original; retain disagreements for review.

  3. Hand over an explicit decision record

    Separate proposed edits, approved wording and completed signatures. Record who decided, when and which version was accepted before updating any downstream workflow.

Illustrative example: an NDA review packet

A fictional team finds a date in an NDA draft that differs from its approved template. It records both source locations, assigns a reviewer and marks the question pending. The document remains unsigned while the authorized reviewer decides which wording to accept.

What this guide covers

This is an editorial planning guide. The inspected landing pages describe proposed capabilities; they do not establish working extraction, legal assessment or signature integrations. Decisions and signatures remain with authorized people.
