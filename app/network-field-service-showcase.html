Follow these three resources in order. Keep the approved scope, evidence references and open questions together in the authorized service record.

Editorial checklist for prerequisites, work observations, exceptions and acceptance evidence. Use it to define what must be recorded before, during and after the visit.

Put the guide into practice

  1. Define the work and its evidence

    Record the exact task, visit sequence, local time zone, access window, expected tests and responsible reviewer. Agree separate measures for response, restoration and resolution.

  2. Complete approvals before commitment

    Verify written availability and the full quoted cost, including taxes, travel, freight and applicable import costs. Obtain formal client confirmation, the client's PO and Zion's specific operational release before any purchase, reservation or dispatch.

  3. Close with reviewed results

    Record actual timestamps, expected and observed test results, exceptions and open items. Keep technician completion separate from written client acceptance, and assign an owner to anything still pending.

Worked example

Illustrative example: replacing a switch

For a fictional switch-replacement visit, the team first defines the connectivity checks and rollback conditions in Operations Evidence. It uses the Handoff checklist to review access, technician evidence and the separate approvals. After authorized work, it records actual tests and exceptions in the Completion Pack. Client acceptance remains pending until the authorized reviewer provides a written response.

What this guide covers

This is a planning guide, not a confirmed service booking. Before any purchase, reservation or dispatch, obtain formal written client confirmation of the exact scope and price, the client's PO and a separate, specific Zion operational release. Availability, coverage and service targets require written verification; resource names alone do not establish working AI, live monitoring or automatic dispatch.
