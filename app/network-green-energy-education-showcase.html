Energy and education: plan a measurable pilot

Start with one question you can evaluate: which building needs an energy review, or which lesson needs clearer evidence of learning? These resources help frame the discussion and identify what a useful pilot would need to demonstrate.

Put the guide into practice

  1. Choose one decision

    Write the question, current process, owner and review date. Select one building, lesson or course so the pilot has a manageable scope.

  2. Define the evidence

    List the input sources, units or rubric, comparison baseline and acceptance criteria. Request a demonstration using suitable sample data before planning an integration.

  3. Review before expanding

    Record the observed result, assumptions, exceptions and reviewer decision. Expand only after the owner has accepted the evidence and any required operational approvals are in place.

Worked example

Illustrative example: a training center

A training center wants to review evening electricity use and one course's lesson structure. It prepares separate sample datasets: weekly meter readings with opening hours, and a lesson plan with its learning objectives. The facilities lead reviews the energy comparison; an educator reviews the curriculum mapping. Their findings remain two independently reviewed pilot results, with no assumed savings or improvement in learning.
